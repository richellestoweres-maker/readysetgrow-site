What makes it different

Most apps hand you a library. This one answers the question you have at 6:40 PM.

A parent holding a screaming baby is not browsing a table of contents. Three things follow from taking that seriously.

Right Now

Help in the moment, not a PDF to read later

Tap what is happening. It is the witching hour. They will not stop crying. Their breathing looks wrong. You get triage first, then something to try in the next sixty seconds, then the explanation.

The rule: the first thing you are asked to do is doable one handed while holding a crying baby.

The schedule builder

One wake time in, the whole day out

Tell it when they woke up. It lays out naps, wake windows, bedtime, and when to start the bath. Baby woke at nine instead of six, the whole day shifts. A nap ran short, the rest recalculates from it.

Said out loud: wake windows are widely used practice guides, not an official standard. Every schedule says so, because nobody should feel they are failing to hit a number that does not exist.

Support lenses

Reordered for your child, never hidden from you

Turn on the support that helps, such as sensory, ADHD, or communication. They stack, and they reorder what surfaces first. A lens can soften how gently a milestone window is described.

The line that never moves: no lens ever hides the guidance to talk with a pediatrician. Reassurance never blocks action.

The gaps

The things nobody brings awareness to.

Every one of these is something parents find out too late, or never. Each is in the app with its sources attached.

Your flange probably does not fit

Pumps ship with 24mm and 27mm because those are middle of the road, not because they fit most people. Measure the nipple only, add 2 to 4mm. It is the fastest fix available to someone struggling, and almost nobody is told.

Frozen milk that smells like soap

High lipase is not spoiled milk, and it is fixable by scalding before freezing. People usually discover it after building a stash of three hundred ounces. Test one bag at five days first.

Retractions, the sign parents are never taught

Take the shirt off and look at the bare chest. Skin sucking in below the neck, under the breastbone, between the ribs. A child can be working extremely hard to breathe while still looking alert.

CMV, and a testing window that closes

The most common infectious cause of birth defects, and most pregnant people have never heard of it. Prevention is largely not sharing food with a toddler. A newborn has to be tested within two to three weeks or it can no longer be confirmed.

Recovery is longer than one appointment

Bleeding for weeks. Night sweats. Hair falling out at month three. Rage rather than sadness. The fourth trimester week by week, with the maternal mental health lines pinned where someone who needs them will actually see them.

And the parent gets looked after too

An app that talks all day to someone exhausted and half convinced they are doing it wrong should say otherwise sometimes. Not empty praise. Specific, about something they actually did.

Where it comes from

Every claim carries its source, and a link straight to it.

We are a parenting app, not a medical authority. The honest way to hold that position is to say where something came from and let you check us in one tap. Each topic also records which version of the guidance it was written against, so it is obvious later what needs rechecking.

Ranges, not deadlines
Milestones use the CDC's 2022 revision, which describes what most children can do by an age rather than the average. Every milestone renders with a window, never a pass or a fail.
Never diagnose
Content says "some children" and "one possibility is". It never says "your child has". Lenses describe support a family wants, not a label on a child.
Safety before explanation
Anything with an emergency version shows the emergency check first. Nobody should scroll past reassurance to learn their baby needs to be seen.
Community wisdom, labeled
Some of what parents pass to each other works and has never been studied. Those entries carry a visible tag saying exactly that, so they are never mistaken for clinical guidance.
